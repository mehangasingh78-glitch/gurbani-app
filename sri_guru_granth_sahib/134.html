ਬਾਰਹਮਾਹਾ ਮਾਝ ਮਹਲਾ 5 ਘਰੁ 4
                ੴਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ॥    
(PAGE 133):     Barah Maah (The Twelve Months) Majh Mehla 5 Ghur(u) 4
                               Ek Onkar Satgur Persad(i)
                                                                       

INTRODUCTION:   Hindu Granths strongly believe in the importance of the timing of events. They believe that the relative positions of the planets affect the fortune of individuals, and there are auspicious and inauspicious moments for important events in their lives. To avoid the negative effects of the planets, the Hindus perform many rituals – for example, give donations to Brahmans; not to eat on certain days; make some unusual offerings to gods; certain dos and don’ts during eclipses; undertake pilgrimages – and so on.  The Hindus believe in the importance of the first day of every month and of the different phases of the moon. With such misbeliefs, they become increasingly lost in doubt and superstition and forget Waheguru completely.  That is totally contrary to the Principles of Guru Granth Sahib, which promotes full belief in Waheguru and total disbelief in the importance of certain days and moments.  For Sikhs, only those moments are auspicious in which one remembers Waheguru, and those in which one forgets Waheguru are inauspicious. Please pay attention to the following holy Shabads:  
ਮਾਰੂ ਮਹਲਾ 1 (1015)॥ ਸਭੇ ਰੁਤੀ ਚੰਗੀਆ ਜਿਤੁ ਸਚੇ ਸਿਉ ਨੇਹੁ॥ ਸਾ ਧਨ ਕੰਤੁ ਪਛਾਣਿਆ ਸੁਖਿ ਸੁਤੀ ਨਿਸਿ ਡੇਹੁ॥
Maru Mehla 1 (1015):  Subhay rooti chungiyaan jit(u) Sachay siyoun nayhou. Sa dhun Kunt(u) puchhaaniya, sookh(i) sooti nis(i) dayhou.
MEANING: All the seasons are delightful in which one loves God. That woman (human bride), who recognizes (makes connection with) the Waheguru-Husband, sleeps (spends life) with comfort, night and day.

ਸਲੋਕ ਮਹਲਾ 5 (318)॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਸੋਈ ਦਿਨਸੁ ਸੁਹਾਵੜਾ ਜਿਤੁ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਆਵੈ ਚਿਤਿ॥ ਜਿਤੁ ਦਿਨਿ ਵਿਸਰੈ ਪਾਰਬ੍ਰਹਮ ਫਿਟੁ ਭਲੇਰੀ ਰੁਤਿ॥
Sloak Mehla 5 (318):  Nanak soyee dinus(u) soohaavrra jit(u) Prubh aavaiy chit(i).  Jit(u) din(i) visraiy Parbrahm, phiitt(u) bhulayri roott(i). 
MEANING: Only that day is pleasant (comforting) on which one remembers Waheguru; and that season is condemnable when one forgets Waheguru, oh Nanak!

ਬਿਲਾਵਲੁ ਮਹਲਾ 3 ਵਾਰ ਸਤ (842)॥ ------ ਆਪੇ ਪੂਰਾ ਕਰੇ ਸੁ ਹੋਇ॥ ਏਹਿ ਥਿਤੀ ਵਾਰ ਦੂਜਾ ਦੋਇ॥ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਬਾਝਹੁ ਅੰਧੁ ਗੁਬਾਰੁ॥ ਥਿਤੀ ਵਾਰ ਸੇਵਹਿ ਮੁਗਧ ਗਵਾਰ॥
Bilawal(u) Mehla 3, Seven Days of the Week (842):  ---------- Aapay Poora kuray su hoyay. Eh(i) thiti vaar dooja doyay. Satgur baajhoun undh(u) goobaar(u). Thiti vaar sayveh(i) moogudh guvaar.  
MEANING:  ---------- Whatever the Perfect Waheguru wishes to do, will certainly happen (superstitions about the timing are baseless).  Superstitions about different phases of the moon and the days of the week sink the believer deeper into the darkness of Maya and create conflicts in the mind (between believing in Waheguru and the fortune tellers about auspicious and inauspicious times).   Without the Satguru, there is complete darkness of ignorance, and only the foolish and the ill-informed believe in the effects of different phases of the moon and the days of the week.
As you can see, Guru Amar Dass Ji calls those people foolish and ill-informed who believe in the importance of certain days and phases of the moon. 
How much more can be said to clarify the meaninglessness (unimportance) of timings of important events in life!!  Nobody can stop events (sad or happy) from happening if Waheguru wishes them to, no matter what rituals one performs. Most Hindus consult fortune tellers to guide them on auspicious or inauspicious times for important events like marriages, buying property, entering a new home, opening a business, and so on.  But their events are as successful or unsuccessful as anybody else’s who do not believe in the importance of certain days or moments.  (With the exception of a few, most of the Westerners have no belief in timings, but they are the most advanced on the planet. So, why believe in timings? In fact, it is a sin for a Sikh to believe in timings. 
In addition to the few holy Shabads given above, there are many more such Shabads in Guru Granth Sahib. In fact, there are many long holy compositions which explain in more detail the insignificance (in fact, negative effects) of believing in the superstitions of timings.  Guru Nanak Sahib has written on “twelve months” and the “phases of the moon”.  Guru Amar Dass Ji has written about the seven days of the week, and Guru Arjun Dev Ji on “twelve months” (the composition under discussion), “day and night”, “phases of the moon” and the six seasons.  Kabir Sahib has also written on the phases of the moon and the seven days of the week.

Now, let us go back to the main holy composition.

(ਅੰਗ 133)॥      ਕਿਰਤਿ ਕਰਮ ਕੇ ਵੀਛੁੜੇ ਕਰਿ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਮੇਲਹੁ ਰਾਮ॥ ਚਾਰਿ ਕੁੰਟ ਦਹਦਿਸਿ ਭ੍ਰਮੇ ਥਕਿ ਆਏ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਕੀ ਸਾਮ॥ ਧੇਨੁ ਦੁਧੈ ਤੇ ਬਾਹਰੀ ਕਿਤੈ ਨ ਆਵੇ ਕਾਮ॥ ਜਲ ਬਿਨੁ ਸਾਖ ਕੁਮਲਾਵਤੀ ਉਪਜਹਿ ਨਾਹੀ ਦਾਮ॥ ਹਰਿ ਨਾਹ ਨ ਮਿਲੀਐ ਸਾਜਨੈ ਕਤ ਪਾਈਐ ਬਿਸਰਾਮ॥ ਜਿਤੁ ਘਰਿ ਹਰਿ ਕੰਤੁ ਨ ਪ੍ਰਗਟਈ ਭਠਿ ਨਗਰ ਸੇ ਗ੍ਰਾਮ॥ ਸ੍ਰਬ ਸੀਗਾਰ ਤੰਬੋਲ ਰਸ ਸਣੁ ਦੇਹੀ ਸਭ ਖਾਮ॥ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਸੁਆਮੀ ਕੰਤ ਵਿਹੂਣੀਆ ਮੀਤ ਸਜਣ ਸਭਿ ਜਾਮ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਕੀ ਬੇਨੰਤੀਆ ਕਰਿ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਦੀਜੈ ਨਾਮੁ॥ ਹਰਿ ਮੇਲਹੁ ਸੁਆਮੀ ਸੰਗਿ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਜਿਸ ਕਾ ਨਿਹਚਲ ਧਾਮ॥ 1॥
(PAGE 133):  Kirt(i) kerm kay veechhurray kur(i) kirpa maylhou Ram.  Chaar(i) koont deh dis(i) bhermay, thuck(i) aayay Prubh kee saam. Dhayn(u) doodhaiy tay bahri, kitaiy na aavay kaam. Jul bin(u) saakh koomlaavti oopjeh(i) naahi daam. Hur(i) Naah na miliyaiy saajnaiy kut paayiyaiy bisraam. Jit ghur(i) Hur(i) kunt(u) na pergutyee bhutth(i) nagar say graam. Serb seegaar tumboal rus, sun(u) dayhi subh khaam. Prubh Soami kunt-vihooniya meet sujjan subh(i) Jaam. Nanak kee baynuntiya kur(i) kirpa deejaiy Naam(u).  Hur(i) maylhou Soami sung(i) Prubh, jis ka nihchul dhaam.   1.
Word Meaning:  Kirt(i) = done.     Kerm = deeds.     Veechhurray = disconnected.     Chaar(i) Koont = the four major directions.   Dehdis(i) = ten sub directions (8 at 45o angles plus vertically up and down).   Bhermay = wandered (without any achievement).   Saam = shelter.     Dhayn(u) = cow (considered sacred by the Hindus).    Baahri = outside of.   Saakh = vegetables; crops.  Koomlaavti = wilts.    Daam = money.    Naah(u) = Lord; Master (husband).   Kut = where, how.   Bisraam = rest; peace of mind.    Ghur(i) = home; heart.    Pergutyee = appears.   Bhutth = kiln; hell.   Seegaar = adornment.   Tumbol = a chewable roll of betel leaves with tobacco in it.   Sun(i) = including.    Khaam = weak; insufficient.   Kunt = husband.   Vihooniyaan = without; devoid of.  Jaam = Jum = angel of death.   Nihchul = permanent; eternal.   Dhaam = residence.     1.
MEANING:  We have been separated from You because of our previous ignoble deeds; please be kind and reconnect us to You, oh Waheguru!  We have exhausted ourselves while wandering in every possible direction in search of happiness, and have finally come to Your shelter.  Just like a cow which does not produce milk, or like a crop which withers away without water and is unable to result in any money, we are worthless without the dear Lord, Waheguru.  How can we find peace without You? The heart, in which Waheguru does not appear, is like a town which is burning like hell. Without You, we are just like a married woman whose husband is away; all beautifications and luxuries, like betel wraps, are useless for her, including her own adorned body.  All the friends and relatives seem like agents of death without You, oh our Lord-husband Waheguru!   I, (Guru) Nanak, make a humble request to You to kindly grant me Your Naam.  Kindly connect me with the Lord whose Residence is eternal, oh Waheguru!         1.
NOTE:  Even though Guru Sahib advises certain dos and don’ts for certain months, they apply to each and every month, in fact, to each and every moment.

ਚੇਤਿ ਗੋਵਿੰਦੁ ਆਰਾਧੀਐ ਹੋਵੈ ਅਨੰਦੁ ਘਣਾ॥ ਸੰਤ ਜਨਾ ਮਿਲਿ ਪਾਈਐ ਰਸਨਾ ਨਾਮੁ ਭਣਾ॥ ਜਿਨਿ ਪਾਇਆ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਆਪਣਾ ਆਏ ਤਿਸਹਿ ਗਣਾ॥ ਇਕੁ ਖਿਨੁ ਤਿਸੁ ਬਿਨੁ ਜੀਵਣਾ ਬਿਰਥਾ ਜਨਮੁ ਜਣਾ॥ ਜਲਿ ਥਲਿ ਮਹੀਅਲਿ ਪੂਰਿਆ ਰਵਿਆ ਵਿਚਿ ਵਣਾ॥ ਸੋ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਚਿਤਿ ਨ ਆਵਈ ਕਿਤੜਾ ਦੁਖੁ ਗਣਾ॥ ਜਿਨੀ ਰਾਵਿਆ ਸੋ ਪ੍ਰਭੂ ਤਿਨਾ ਭਾਗੁ ਮਣਾ॥ ਹਰਿ ਦਰਸਨ ਕੰਉ ਮਨੁ ਲੋਚਦਾ ਨਾਨਕ ਪਿਆਸ ਮਨਾ॥ ਚੇਤਿ ਮਿਲਾਏ ਸੋ ਪ੍ਰਭੂ ਤਿਸ ਕੈ ਪਾਇ ਲਗਾ॥ 2॥
Chayt(i) Govind(u) araadhiyaiy, hovaiy anund(u) ghuna. Sunt junaa mil(i) paayiyaiy rusna Naam(u) bhunaa. Jin(i) paaya Prubh(u) aapna, aayay tiseh(i) gunaa. Ikk(u) khin(u) Tis(u) bin(u) jeevna, birtha junum(u) junaa. Jul(i) thul(i) muhhiul(i) pooriya, ruviya vich(i) vunaa. So Prubh chit(i) na aavuyee kitrra dookh(u) gunaaa. Jinhi raaviya so Prubh(u) tinnah bhaag(u) munaa. Hur(i) dershun kuoun mun(u) loachda, Nanak piyaas munaa.  Chayt(i) milaayay so Prubhoo tis kaiy paayay lugaan.   2.
Word Meaning:  Chayt(i) = in (the month of) Chayt(u).   Anund = blissfulness.  Ghuna = immense.   Rusna = tongue.   Bhuna = utter.  Guna = consider (worthy of).   Khin(u) = a small moment.    Birtha = empty.  Junaa = consider.   Muhiul(i) = in the sky.    Pooriya = has filled.    Ruviya = present.   Vuna = forests.   Raaviya = enjoyed.   Munaa = shine of fortune on the forehead.    Loachda = longs for.   Piyaas = thirst.        2.
MEANING:  If one remembers Waheguru in the month of Chayt(u), he enjoys bliss.  Waheguru can be realized by reciting His Naam with the tongue in the company of the Guru. Only those who are considered to have really been born (their lives are successful) are those who connect with Waheguru.  To live without Him even for a moment is like wasting one’s whole life. If one does not remember Waheguru, which is present in waters, on the land, and in the sky, he suffers countless troubles (sufferings).   But only those who enjoy union with Waheguru have good fortune on their foreheads.  My mind is thirsty for the sight of Waheguru and longs to see Him.  I will drop on the feet (serve him like a slave) of the holy devotee (Satguru) who connects me with Waheguru in the month of Chayt(u) (any time).          2.

ਵੈਸਾਖਿ ਧੀਰਨਿ ਕਿਉ ਵਾਢੀਆਂ ਜਿਨਾ ਪ੍ਰੇਮ ਬਿਛੋਹੁ॥ ਹਰਿ ਸਾਜਨੁ ਪੁਰਖੁ ਵਿਸਾਰਿ ਕੈ ਲਗੀ ਮਾਇਆ ਧੋਹੁ॥ ਪੁਤ੍ਰ ਕਲਤ੍ਰ ਨ ਸੰਗਿ ਧਨਾ ਹਰਿ ਅਵਿਨਾਸੀ ਓਹੁ॥ ਪਲਚਿ ਪਲਚਿ ਸਗਲੀ ਮੁਈ ਝੂਠੈ ਧੰਧੈ ਮੋਹੁ॥ ਇਕਸੁ ਹਰਿ ਕੇ ਨਾਮ ਬਿਨੁ ਅਗੈ ਲਈਅਹਿ ਖੋਹਿ॥ ਦਯੁ ਵਿਸਾਰਿ ਵਿਗੁਚਣਾ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਬਿਨੁ ਅਵਰੁ ਨ ਕੋਇ॥ ਪ੍ਰੀਤਮ ਚਰਣੀ ਜੋ ਲਗੇ ਤਿਨ ਕੀ ਨਿਰਮਲ ਸੋਇ॥
