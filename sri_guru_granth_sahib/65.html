NOTE:  Even though Waheguru is omnipresent and lives in every heart, we are unable to see Him because there is a thick curtain of Maya between Him and us. This curtain is called the sea of Maya or the world-sea. So, Gurbani professes that we have to swim across this sea to be one with Him. Another construct used in Gurbani is that Waheguru resides above the effects of Maya; since the visible world is Maya, God is thought to live way up high in the sky above all the visible universe. So, to reach Him, we have to rise very high too (above the effects of Maya). So, for our understanding, Guru Sahiban have used the analogy of climbing up a mountain to reach Waheguru. However, the reasoning of some religions that believe God lives somewhere up in the sky differs from that of Gurbani.  Because God is inaccessible, they think He must be living high up in the sky. For example, Muslims believe there are seven skies above the earth, and that God lives on the seventh. Similarly, Hindus believe that their gods live in paradise, which is believed to be somewhere in the sky. So, please note the difference in reasoning. 
MEANING:  The sea of Maya is very scary because it is very difficult to swim across it, oh my brothers. However, if one can connect with the Perfect Guru by virtue of one’s love for Him, He can foster Waheguru’s Naam in his heart, which can take him across this sea.     Pause.
Looking at the tough and challenging climbing of mountain-high path (to the residence of Waheguru), the human bride feels intimidated in this world because the tall mountain path is tough, and the bride has no ladder to climb. However, because of the Guru’s union with Waheguru, the devotee of the Guru realizes that God is in his mind and is thus able to swim across the sea of Maya.         1.        If I realize that I have to leave this world because I am mortal (and so is the rest of the world) and only God and the Guru are immortal, the best thing for me to do is to sing Waheguru’s praise in the company of the devotees to become “one” with Waheguru, which is the objective of human life.            2.          A man may have beautiful mansions, thousands of fortified forts, many, many horses, and elephants with (decorated) saddles, and countless soldiers, none of these accompany him (at death), even though the ignorant man might have experienced lots of hardships to acquire them.         3.
One accumulates gold and silver (with great effort); and he may be well known for his riches in his area, but he does not realize that all the wealth becomes a bigger net for immoral activities because of his further attraction for Maya, and the sword of death is always hanging on his head without Waheguru’s Naam. When the body collapses, the game of the mind is over, and he must suffer the consequences of his ignoble deeds.       4.
One feels overjoyed to see his sons and his wife on his bed. To look attractive, one wears nice clothes and sprays perfumes, like the extract of roses and sandalwood, but the individual has to leave his house and the belongings at his death, and the body is made of earth, mixed with dust (thus all adornments become useless).          5.
During one’s life, one may be called a chief, a king, the ruler of a small state, or a chieftain, but all such titles become a source of conceit, due to which the individual suffers loss of peace of mind.  The follower of his mind forgets Waheguru, and looks like a reed burnt in a forest fire (short- tempered – burnt by the fire of desires).         6.
This world is black like kohl due to the darkness (ignorance) (caused by Maya). Anybody who falls in the trap of Maya will turn black by body and mind due to his haumein, and will leave this world as such (full of haumein) (and will be punished in Waheguru’s Court).  Only Guru’s Shabad can put out the fire of desires (for Maya), and salvage people.       7.
The followers of their mind die in the world-sea suffering needlessly from the effects of Maya, but the devotees swim across this unfathomable sea.  I pray that I may not drop Waheguru’s Naam from my mind, and continue to deal in the Naam, which is precious like a jewel. Waheguru is the King of kings, and one can swim the sea of Maya with His True Naam, oh Nanak.   8. 16.

MEANING:  (When every visible thing is perishable), how can this world be called a permanent place to live, oh, man?  (If you want to be immortal and want to live forever, you need to be “one” with God because only He is immortal). (To be one with Him) You need to have faith in Him, do noble deeds, and stay connected with Waheguru (His Naam). This Naam-wealth will serve as your expense for the journey to Waheguru’s.      Pause.
One cannot think of this world as his permanent place, because he is aware of the fear of impending death, which always haunts him.  Man’s stay here can be thought of as permanent only if the whole world is permanent (and it is not).         1.
(Disregarding the fact that the world is not permanent, people still act as if this were so, and they forget God completely.  For example, to acquire supernatural powers, the yogi sits in a special position for a long time, and the mullah sits on his designated seat to preach to others.  Likewise, the Pundits explain their religious books to people, and the Sidhs preach in temples (as if they are going to live forever).         2.        The gods, the Sidhs, assistants of Shiva, Gundherbs, the Moonies, Peers, the learned men, and the chiefs, all leave this world turn by turn, while the others have to leave (sooner, or later).        3. 
The rulers – big and small, the chiefs, and the rich, all have left the world. You have to understand that you too have to leave in a short time, oh my mind.        4.
Many tell you that (this world is not permanent), but that is only in words, because only a few really understand this fact.  (Guru) Nanak respectfully submits that only God is eternal, present in water, on earth, and in the sky.        5.       The whole world is a temporary stay because living beings come and go; only incomprehensible, unreachable, compassionate, the creator of the entire universe, and the source of all gifts – Waheguru - is permanent (eternal).         6. 
The only permanent entity in this universe is the one that has no accountability (the burden of immoral deeds) on its head (in other words, which is like Waheguru).  Even the sky and the earth are perishable; only the One (God) is eternal.        7.
The sun, the days, the moon, the nights, and millions of stars will all disappear; the truth is that the only eternal power is Waheguru, oh Nanak.    8. 17.
