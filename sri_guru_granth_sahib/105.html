(ਅੰਗ 104)॥        ਆਸ ਮਨੋਰਥੁ ਪੂਰਨੁ ਹੋਵੈ ਭੇਟਤ ਗੁਰ ਦਰਸਾਇਆ ਜੀਉ॥ 2॥ ਅਗਮ ਅਗੋਚਰ ਕਿਛੁ ਮਿਤਿ ਨਹੀ ਜਾਨੀ॥ ਸਾਧਿਕ ਸਿਧ ਧਿਆਵਹਿ ਗਿਆਨੀ॥ ਖੁਦੀ ਮਿਟੀ ਚੂਕਾ ਭੋਲਾਵਾ ਗੁਰਿ ਮਨ ਹੀ ਮਹਿ ਪ੍ਰਗਟਾਇਆ ਜੀਉ॥ 3॥ ਅਨਦ ਮੰਗਲ ਕਲਿਆਣ ਨਿਧਾਨਾ॥ ਸੂਖ ਸਹਜ ਹਰਿ ਨਾਮੁ ਵਖਾਨਾ॥ ਹੋਇ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲੁ ਸੁਆਮੀ ਅਪਨਾ ਨਾਉ ਨਾਨਕ ਘਰ ਮਹਿ ਆਇਆ ਜੀਉ॥  4॥25॥32॥
Majh Mehla 5:  Jea praan Prubh muneh(i) adhaara.  Bhugat jeeveh(i) goon gaayay Apaara. Goon nidhaan Amrit(u) Hur(i) Naama, Hur(i) dhiyayay dhiyayay sookh(u) paaya jeeo. 1. Munsa dhaar(i) jo ghur(i) tay aavaiy. Sadh sung(i) junum(u) mern(u) mitaavaiy. 
(PAGE 104):             Aas munoruth(u) poorun(u) hovaiy, bhaytut Gur dersaaya jeeo. 2. Agumm Agochur, kichh(u) mit(i) nuhee jaani.  Saadhik sidh dhiyaveh(i) giyani.  Khoodi mitee chooka bholaava, Gur(i) mun hee meh(i) pergtaaya jeeo.  Anund mungul kalyaan nidhaana. Sookh sehj Hur(i) Naam(u) vukhaana. Hoyay kirpal(u) Soami apna, Naoun Nanak ghur meh(i) aaya jeeo.  4.25.32.
Word Meaning:  Jea Praan = soul; life.   Adhaara = support.    Apaara = (of) limitless (Waheguru).   Nidhaan = treasure.   Dhiyayay = remembering (God’s Naam).       1.
Munsa = wish.   Dhaar(i) = conceive; decide.   Munoruth(u) = objective.  Bhaytut = (on) meeting.   Dersaaya = sight.        2.      Agumm = unreachable.   Agochar = incomprehensible. =   Mit(i) = measurements; value.  Saadhik = those under training to focus their mind.   Sidh = Yogis who have acquired some supernatural power by focusing their mind.   Giyaani = having Divine Knowledge.    Khoodi = selfishness; ego.    Bholaava = doubts; misbeliefs.   Pergtaaya = made visible; enlightened (the mind).           3.
Anund Mungal Kalyan = blissfulness; joyful mind.   Nidhana = treasure; source.   Sehj = spiritual state where Maya has no effect.     Vukhaana = recite.    4.    
MEANING:  Waheguru is the support for life and the mind.  His devotees survive (spiritually) by singing the virtues of the limitless Waheguru.  His Naam is the treasure of virtues and of Amrit; one finds huge comfort in reciting His Naam.        1.
In the company of the Guru (or His holy congregation), all one’s conceived wishes are fulfilled, and the cycle of reincarnation is broken. On seeing and meeting the Guru (spiritually connecting with Him), all one’s wishes and objectives are realized.         2.
Even though the followers of the Yogic path, the Sidh yogis, and the Giyanis recite His Naam, nobody can determine the value (magnanimity) of the unreachable and the inconceivable Waheguru. But the Guru has enlightened my mind, and all my doubts and misbeliefs have been cleared.          3.           Waheguru is the treasure of all the comforts and bliss; one achieves all the comforts and the higher spiritual state (where Maya has no effect) by reciting His Naam.  With His benevolence, I have acquired His Naam in my heart, oh Nanak.     4.25.32.

ਮਾਝ ਮਹਲਾ 5॥ ਸੁਣਿ ਸੁਣਿ ਜੀਵਾ ਸੋਇ ਤੁਮਾਰੀ॥ ਤੂ ਪ੍ਰੀਤਮੁ ਠਾਕੁਰੁ ਅਤਿ ਭਾਰੀ॥ ਤੁਮਰੇ ਕਰਤਬ ਤੁਮ ਹੀ ਜਾਣਹੁ ਤੁਮਰੀ ਓਟ ਗੁੋਪਾਲਾ ਜੀਉ॥ 1॥ ਗੁਣ ਗਾਵਤ ਮਨੁ ਹਰਿਆ ਹੋਵੈ॥ ਕਥਾ ਸੁਣਤ ਮਲੁ ਸਗਲੀ ਖੋਵੈ॥ ਭੇਟਤ ਸੰਗਿ ਸਾਧ ਸੰਤਨ ਕੈ ਸਦਾ ਜਪਉ ਦਇਆਲਾ ਜੀਉ॥ 2॥ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਅਪੁਨਾ ਸਾਸਿ ਸਾਸਿ ਸਮਾਰਉ॥ ਇਹ ਮਤਿ ਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ ਮਨਿ ਧਾਰਉ॥ ਤੁਮਰੀ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਤੇ ਮਨਿ ਹੋਇ ਪ੍ਰਗਾਸਾ ਸਰਬ ਮਇਆ ਪ੍ਰਤਿਪਾਲਾ ਜੀਉ॥ 3॥ ਸਤਿ ਸਤਿ ਸਤਿ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਸੋਈ॥ ਸਦਾ ਸਦਾ ਸਦ ਆਪੇ ਹੋਈ॥ ਚਲਿਤ ਤੁਮਾਰੇ ਪ੍ਰਗਟ ਪਿਆਰੇ ਦੇਖਿ ਨਾਨਕ ਭਏ ਨਿਹਾਲਾ ਜੀਉ॥ 4॥26॥33॥
Majh Mehla 5:  Soon(i) soon(i) jeevaan soyay Toomaari. Tu Preetum(u), Tthaakur(u) ut(i) bhaari. Toomray kertub Toom hee jaanhou, Toomri oat Gopala jeeo. 1. Goon gaavut mun(u) huriya hovaiy.  Kutha soonut mul(u) sugli khovaiy. Bhaytut sung(i) Sadh Suntan kaiy, suda jupoun Duyyala jeeo. 2.  Prubh(u) apuna saas(i) saas(i) smaaroun. Eh mut(i) Gur persad(i) mun(i) dhaaroun. Toomri kirpa tay mun(i) hoyay pergaasa, serb muyya Pritpaala jeeo.  3. Sat(i) Sat(i) Sat(i) Prubh(u) soyee.  Suda suda, sud Aapay hoyee Chulit Toomaaray pergut Piyaray, daykh(i) Nanak bhuyyay nihaala jeeo.  4.26.33.
Word Meaning:   Soyay = (Sobha); fame, praise; information.   Tthaakur(u) = Lord.   Ut(i) = extremely.   Bhaari = great.   Kertub = amazing feats.   Oat = shelter.     1.
Huriya = green (comes to life).   Kutha = narration (of virtues).   Mul(u) = filth.   Sugli = all.     Khovaiy = lose; wash away.    Bhaytut = on meeting (in the company).       2.
Smaaroun = remember.    Dhaaroun = acquire; adopt.    Pergaasa = enlightenment.  
Muyya = kindness.  Pritpaala = raising, looking after.        3.
Sat(i) = True; eternal.     Chulit = bewitching (captivating) feats.   Pergut = visible; appear.    Nihaala = blissful.      4.
MEANING:  I survive (spiritually) only by listening to Your praise, oh Waheguru!  You are my magnanimous darling, Lord.  Only You understand Your captivating feats; I am only in Your shelter, oh the provider for the world, Waheguru!           1.         My mind blooms when I sing Your praise, and all the filth of my mind is washed away when I listen to the narration of Your virtues. I recite You Naam in the company of the holy saints, oh kind Waheguru!            2.
I recite the Naam of my Waheguru with every breath.  I acquired this wisdom through the Guru's kindness. My heart is enlightened with Your grace, oh the provider for the whole world, Waheguru! You are kind to everybody.          3.
Only Waheguru is eternal, and He alone is there all the time.  His amazing feats are visible everywhere, and I feel blissful on seeing them, oh Nanak!   4. 26.33.

ਮਾਝ ਮਹਲਾ 5॥ ਹੁਕਮੀ ਵਰਸਣ ਲਾਗੇ ਮੇਹਾ॥ ਸਾਜਨ ਸੰਤ ਮਿਲਿ ਨਾਮੁ ਜਪੇਹਾ॥ ਸੀਤਲ ਸਾਤਿ ਸਹਜ ਸੁਖੁ ਪਾਇਆ ਠਾਢਿ ਪਾਈ ਪ੍ਰਭਿ ਆਪੇ ਜੀਉ॥ 1॥ ਸਭੁ ਕਿਛੁ ਬਹੁਤੋ ਬਹੁਤੁ ਉਪਾਇਆ॥ ਕਰਿ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਪ੍ਰਭਿ ਸਗਲ ਰਜਾਇਆ॥ ਦਾਤਿ ਕਰਹੁ ਮੇਰੇ ਦਾਤਾਰਾ ਜੀਅ ਜੰਤ ਸਭਿ ਧ੍ਰਾਪੇ ਜੀਉ॥ 2॥ ਸਚਾ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਸਚੀ ਨਾਈ॥ ਗੁਰ ਪਰਸਾਦਿ ਤਿਸੁ ਸਦਾ ਧਿਆਈ॥ ਜਨਮ ਮਰਣ ਭੈ ਕਾਟੇ ਮੋਹਾ ਬਿਨਸੇ ਸੋਗ ਸੰਤਾਪੇ ਜੀਉ॥ 3॥ ਸਾਸਿ ਸਾਸਿ ਨਾਨਕੁ ਸਾਲਾਹੇ॥ ਸਿਮਰਤ ਨਾਮੁ ਕਾਟੇ ਸਭਿ ਫਾਹੇ॥ ਪੂਰਨ ਆਸ ਕਰੀ ਖਿਨ ਭੀਤਰਿ ਹਰਿ ਹਰਿ ਹਰਿ ਗੁਣ ਜਾਪੇ ਜੀਉ॥ 4॥27॥34॥
Majh Mehla 5:  Huqami versun laagay mayha. Saajun Sunt mil(i) Naam(u) jupayha. Seetal shaant(i) sehj sookh(u) paaya, tthaandh(i) payee Prubh(i) aapay jeeo. 1. Subh(u) kichh(u) buhouto buhout(u) ooapaaya.  Kur(i) kirpa Prubh(i) sugal rujaaya. Daat(i) kurhou mayray Dataara, jea junt subh(i) dhraapay jeeo. 2.  Sacha Sahib Sachi Naayee. Gur persad(i) Tis(u) suda dhiyaayeen.  Junum mern bhaiy kaatay moha, binsay soag suntaapay jeeo. 3.  Saas(i) saas(i) Nanak(u) salaahay.  Simrut Naam(u) kaatay subh(i) phaahay.  Poorun aas kurie khin bheetur(i), Hur(i) Hur(i) Hur(i) goon jaapay jeeo. 4.27.34.
Word Meaning:   Versay = rain (of Waheguru’s Naam) falling.   Mayha = cloud.  Seetal = cool.  Shaant(i) = peaceful.   Sehj = higher spiritual state.  Tthaandh(i) = coolness; peace.     1.
Rujaaya = fed to satisfaction.  Daat = gift; largesse.   Dhraapay = overfed.     2. 
Naayee = greatness.  Moha = attachment.   Binsay = ended.   Suntaap = troubles; tough times.       3.        Phaahay = bonds; shackles.   Khin = moment.   Bheetur(i) = inside.      4.
MEANING:  When all the devotee friends recite Waheguru’s Naam together with dedication, it feels as if His Naam begins to rain in the hearts of the devotees by His Huqam (pleasure).  The mind (suffering from the heat of vile, lust, etc.) begins to feel calm and peaceful, and the comfort of the higher spiritual level (where Maya has no effect) begins to be felt with His kindness.           1          The kind God has created an excess of everything so that all the living beings are completely satisfied.  The provider Waheguru bestows such great largesse that all living beings are fully content.            2.          Waheguru is eternal, and so is His magnanimity; one should always remember Him in Guru’s shelter. With recitation of Waheguru’s Naam, all the fear of reincarnation, attachment with worldly things, and all the troubles and grief are completely eradicated.          3.          I remember Waheguru with every breath as a result of which all the worldly bonds are cut, oh Nanak!   When a person sings the virtues of Waheguru, all his wishes are granted in no time.       4.27.34.
NOTE: Punjab has always been an important agrarian state, but at the time of Guru Sahiban, there were no canals to irrigate the fields.  The farmers depended mainly on rainfall for growing their crops. Not only that, rains also brought relief from the intense heat of May and June. In this Shabad, Guru Sahib uses the analogy of rain's importance to living beings to emphasize the importance of Waheguru’s Naam.
