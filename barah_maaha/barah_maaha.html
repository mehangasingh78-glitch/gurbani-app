ਤੁਖਾਰੀ ਛੰਤ ਮਹਲਾ 1

ਬਾਰਹਮਾਹਾ ੴ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ॥

Tookhari Chhunt Mehla 1, Barhmaha

Ek Onkar Satgur Persad(i)

 

NOTE: The Brahmans in India created a system of superstitions about the auspiciousness and inauspiciousness of certain days to force people to make rich donations.  The first day of every month of the Indian calendar (called Sangraand) was also one of them. Guru Nanak Sahib preached strongly against such superstitions to educate people, and wrote this beautiful and highly enlightening set of verses to tell them that every day, and every moment that one spends in singing praise of the Almighty, Waheguru, is auspicious, and the day or the moment when one forgets Waheguru is inauspicious.  This issue was so important that the Fifth Guru, Guru Arjun Dev Ji also wrote a similar composition later in Raag Majh, to emphasize the hollowness of such misgivings. The beauty of each month that Guru Sahib has described in this composition infuses love for Waheguru in the minds of the people. Enjoy this beautiful holy composition. 

 

(ਅੰਗ 1107)॥ ਤੂ ਸੁਣਿ ਕਿਰਤ ਕਰੰਮਾ ਪੁਰਬਿ ਕਮਾਇਆ॥ ਸਿਰਿ ਸਿਰਿ ਸੁਖ ਸਹੰਮਾ ਦੇਹਿ ਸੁ ਤੂ ਭਲਾ॥ ਹਰਿ ਰਚਨਾ ਤੇਰੀ ਕਿਆ ਗਤਿ ਮੇਰੀ ਹਰਿ ਬਿਨੁ ਘੜੀ ਨ ਜੀਵਾ॥ ਪ੍ਰਿਅ ਬਾਝੁ ਦੁਹੇਲੀ ਕੋਇ ਨ ਬੇਲੀ ਗੁਰਮੁਖਿ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤੁ ਪੀਵਾ॥ ਰਚਨਾ ਰਾਚਿ ਰਹੇ ਨਿਰੰਕਾਰੀ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਮਨਿ ਕਰਮ ਸੁਕਰਮਾ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਪੰਥੁ ਨਿਹਾਲੇ ਸਾ ਧਨ ਤੂ ਸੁਣਿ ਆਤਮ ਰਾਮਾ॥ 1॥

(PAGE 1107): Tu soon(i) kirt kerma, poorub(i) kmaaya. Sir(i), sir(i), sookh sehuma, dayh(i) su Tu, bhulaa.  Hur(i) ruchna Tayri, kiyya gut(i) mayri, Hur(i) bin ghurri na jeevaan.  Priyay Baajh(u) doohayli, koyay na bayli, Gurmukh(i) Amrit(u) peevaan.  Ruchna raach(i) ruhaay Nirunkaari, Prubh mun(i) kerm sookerma.  Nanak, punth(u) nihaalay sa dhun, Tu soon(i) Aatum Rama.  1.

Word Meaning:  Kirt Kerma = (consequences of ) the deeds done.   Poorub(i) Kmaaya = done in previous live.  Sir(i) Sir(i) = respectively.  Sehuma = worries.      Bhulaa = good. Ruchna = (attractions of) Your creations.  Gut(i) = (pitiable) situation.  Priayay = Dear (Waheguru).    Doohaaayli = miserable.  Bayli = friend.    Raach(i) = engrossed.   Nirunkaari = of Waheguru.  Sookerma = the supreme act.  Punth(u) = path.  Sa dhun = that woman (devotee).    Aatum Rama = Oh, Lord.   1.

MEANING:  Everybody reaps the rewards of his previous deeds – some live in comfort, while others live in worries.  I accept Your Will as being good for me, oh Waheguru.  I am engrossed in the attractions of Your creation, so what will my spiritual situation be like!! I do not want to live without You, even for a single moment.  I have no friend, and feel miserable without my Dear (Waheguru).  But I can drink Waheguru’s Naam-Amrit in Guru’s shelter. Everybody is engrossed in the creation of Waheguru, but keeping Him in mind is the supreme deed of all.  Such a devotee begs for His sight and watches the path leading to the union with Waheguru, oh Nanak.     1.             

 

ਬਾਬੀਹਾ ਪ੍ਰਿਉ ਬੋਲੇ ਕੋਕਿਲ ਬਾਣੀਆ॥ ਸਾ ਧਨ ਸਭਿ ਰਸ ਚੋਲੈ ਅੰਕਿ ਸਮਾਣੀਆ॥ ਹਰਿ ਅੰਕਿ ਸਮਾਣੀ ਜਾ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਭਾਣੀ ਸਾ ਸੋਹਾਗਣਿ ਨਾਰੇ॥ ਨਵ ਘਰ ਥਾਪਿ ਮਹਲ ਘਰੁ ਊਚਉ ਨਿਜ ਘਰਿ ਵਾਸੁ ਮੁਰਾਰੇ॥ ਸਭ ਤੇਰੀ ਤੂ ਮੇਰਾ ਪ੍ਰੀਤਮੁ ਨਿਸਿ ਬਾਸੁਰ ਰੰਗਿ ਰਾਵੈ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਪ੍ਰਿਉ ਪ੍ਰਿਉ ਚਵੈ ਬਾਬੀਹਾ ਕੋਕਿਲ ਸਬਦਿ ਸੁਹਾਵੈ॥  2॥

Baabeeha priyo boalay, kokil baaniya.  Sa dhun subh(i) rus choalaiy, unk(i) smaaniya. Hur(i) unk(i) smaani, ja Prubh bhaani, sa sohaagun(i) naaray.  Nuv ghur thaap(i), Mehl, Ghur(u) Oochou, nij ghur(i) vaas(u) Mooraaray. Subh Tayri, Tu mayra Preetum(u), nis(i), baasur rung(i) raavaiy. Nanak, priyo, priyo chuvvaiy baabeeha, kokil Shabad(i) soohaavaiy.  2.

Word Meaning:  Baabiha = Chaatrik = the rain-bird which is believed to drink only the first drop of rain.  Priyo = the call of the baabiha.   Kokil = a songbird with a sweet voice; cuckoo.      Baaniya = sweet voice.   Choalaiy = enjoys.   Unk(i) Smaaniya = becomes part of; merges into.   Bhaani = liked by.  Sohaagun(i) = married to (Waheguru); loved by. Him.  Nuv Ghur = (the human body) with nine doors.  Thaap(i) = by fixing (bringing under control).  Ghur(u) Oochou = high residence; high in transcendence. Vaas(u) = dwelling.  Mooraray = (of) Waheguru.  Nis(i) = night.  Baasur = day. Rung(i)  Raavaiy = recites with love.   2. 

MEANING:  Listening to the love-filled calls of the rain-bird, and the sweet songs of the songbird, the devotee, who is prompted to sing Waheguru’s praise with affection, merges into Him, and enjoys all the sweet tastes of life. But only that devotee merges into Waheguru whom He likes, and she is loved by Waheguru.  Waheguru’s Residence is very high (transcendental), and is not accessible; only that devotee attains that height of transcendence to dwell in Waheguru who controls all nine of her body doors (sensual pleasures), and attains the noble qualities of Waheguru.  She knows that Waheguru belongs to His whole creation, but she loves Him as if He belongs only to her, and remembers Him lovingly day and night. The love-filled call of the rainbird and the sweet songs of the songbird prompt the devotee to sing the Lord’s praise, oh Nanak.  2.

 

ਤੂ ਸੁਣਿ ਹਰਿ ਰਸ ਭਿੰਨੇ ਪ੍ਰੀਤਮ ਆਪਣੇ॥ ਮਨਿ ਤਨਿ ਰਵਤ ਰਵੰਨੇ ਘੜੀ ਨ ਬੀਸਰੈ॥ ਕਿਉ ਘੜੀ ਬਿਸਾਰੀ ਹਉ ਬਲਿਹਾਰੀ ਹਉ ਜੀਵਾ ਗੁਣ ਗਾਏ॥ ਨਾ ਕੋਈ ਮੇਰਾ ਹਉ ਕਿਸੁ ਕੇਰਾ ਹਰਿ ਬਿਨੁ ਰਹਣੁ ਨ ਜਾਏ॥ ਓਟ ਗਹੀ ਹਰਿ ਚਰਣ ਨਿਵਾਸੇ ਭਏ ਪਵਿਤ੍ਰ ਸਰੀਰਾ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਦ੍ਰਿਸਟਿ ਦੀਰਘ ਸੁਖੁ ਪਾਵੈ ਗੁਰ ਸਬਦੀ ਮਨੁ ਧੀਰਾ॥ 3॥

Tu soon(i) Hur(i) rus-bhinnay Preetum apnay.  Mun(i), tun(i), ruvt ruvunnaiy, ghurree na bisraiy. Kiyoun ghurree bisaareen, huoun bulihaari, huoun jeevaam goon gaayay.  Na koyee mayra, huoun kis(u) kayra, Hur(i) bin(u) rehn(u) na jaayay. Oat guhhi, Hur(i) Churn nivaasay, bhuyyay puvittar sreera. Nanak, Drisht(i) deerugh sookh(u) paavaiy Gur Shabadi mun(u) dheera. 3.

Word Meaning:  Rus-Bhinnay = soaked in sweetness. Ruvut Ruvunnaiy = sing praise of the praise-worthy (Waheguru).   Kayra = of.     Oat Guhhi = took shelter.  Drisht(i) = with kind sight.       Deerugh = long; great.  Dheera = peaceful.    3.

MEANING:  Listen to my prayer that I should not forget You even for a single moment, and should recite Your Naam by my body (tongue) and my mind, oh, my sweetness-soaked Waheguru. How can I forget You, even for a short moment, when I survive only by singing Your praise, and am always a sacrifice for You?  Nobody is mine, and I belong to nobody forever. I cannot survive without Waheguru. I took His shelter, and embedded His Feet (His Naam) in my heart because of which my body (and mind) have been purified. I enjoy long (eternal) comfort with Waheguru’s kind sight on me, and have achieved peace of mind (does not wander any more) with Guru’s Shabad, oh Nanak. 3.

 

ਬਰਸੈ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤ ਧਾਰ ਬੂੰਦ ਸੁਹਾਵਣੀ॥ ਸਾਜਨ ਮਿਲੇ ਸਹਜਿ ਸੁਭਾਇ ਹਰਿ ਸਿਉ ਪ੍ਰੀਤਿ ਬਣੀ॥ ਹਰਿ ਮੰਦਰਿ ਆਵੈ ਜਾ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਭਾਵੈ ਧਨ ਊਭੀ ਗੁਣ ਸਾਰੀ॥ ਘਰਿ ਘਰਿ ਕੰਤੁ ਰਵੈ ਸੋਹਾਗਣਿ ਹਉ ਕਿਉ ਕੰਤਿ ਵਿਸਾਰੀ॥ ਊਨਵਿ ਘਨ ਛਾਏ ਬਰਸੁ ਸੁਭਾਏ ਮਨਿ ਤਨਿ ਪ੍ਰੇਮ ਸੁਖਾਵੈ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਵਰਸੈ ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤ ਬਾਣੀ ਕਰਿ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਘਰਿ ਆਵੈ॥ 4॥

Bersaiy Amrit dhaar, boond soohaavni.  Saajun milay sehj(i) soobhaayay, Hur(i) siyoun preet(i) bunee. Hur(i) mundar(i) aavaiy, ja Prubh bhaavaiy, dhun oobhi Goon saari.     chhaayay, bers(u) soobhaayay, mun(i), tun(i) praym sookhaavaiy. Nanak, versaiy Amrit Bani, kur(i) kirpa ghur(i) aavaiy.  4. 

Word Meaning:   Bersaiy = rains.  Dhaar = stream.  Boond = drops.  Soohaavni = pleasing; comforting.     Sehj(i) = in peaceful state.     Soobhaayay = with love.   Mundar(i) = house; heart.  Oobhi = standing; zestfully.  Goon Saari = remembers Virtues.  Ruvaiy = enjoys.  Kunt(i) = by the husband  (Waheguru).   Oonuv(i) = very low.  Ghun(u) = clouds. Chhaayay = overcast.  Sookhaavaiy = provides comfort (bliss).   4.

MEANING:   Waheguru-Friend appears in the heart of the devotee who recites His Naam with deep love and dedication, and the drops of the Naam-Amrit begin to rain as a small stream in his tenth door (mind).  Waheguru appears in a devotee’s heart only if He likes her when she sings His praise zestfully.  All fortunate devotees enjoy union with Waheguru in their heart (mind), but I am an unfortunate, and do not know why He has abandoned (forgotten) me.  Like a cloud, which comes close to the earth to cause rain, Waheguru causes rain of His Amrit-Naam in a devotee’s heart affectionately when He becomes benevolent to her, and takes dwelling in her heart; the devotee feels blissfulness in her body and mind because of Waheguru’s love for her, oh Nanak.    4.

 

ਚੇਤ ਬਸੰਤ ਭਲਾ ਭਵਰ ਸੁਹਾਵੜੇ॥

(ਅੰਗ 1108)॥    ਬਨ ਫੂਲੇ ਮੰਝ ਬਾਰਿ ਮੈ ਪਿਰੁ ਘਰਿ ਬਾਹੁੜੈ॥ ਪਿਰੁ ਘਰਿ ਆਵੈ ਧਨ ਕਿਉ ਸੁਖੁ ਪਾਵੈ ਬਿਰਹਿ ਬਿਰੋਧ ਤਨੁ ਛੀਜੈ॥ ਕੋਕਿਲ ਅੰਬਿ ਸੁਹਾਵੀ ਬੋਲੈ ਕਿਉ ਦੂਖੁ ਅੰਕਿ ਸਹੀਜੈ॥ ਭਵਰੁ ਭਵੰਤਾ ਫੂਲੀ ਡਾਲੀ ਕਿਉ ਜੀਵਾ ਮਰੁ ਮਾਏ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਚੇਤਿ ਸਹਜਿ ਸੁਖੁ ਪਾਵੈ ਜੋ ਹਰਿ ਵਰੁ ਘਰਿ ਧਨ ਪਾਏ॥ 5॥

Chayt, basunt bhulaa, bhuvur soohaavrray. 

(PAGE 1108):          Bun phoolay munjh baar(i), mein Pir(u) ghur(i) baahourraiy.  Pir(u) ghur(i) aavaiy, dhun kiyoun sookh paavaiy, bireh(i) birodh tun(u) chheejaiy.  Kokil umb(i) soohaavi boalaiy, kiyoun dookh(u) unk(i) suheejaiy.  Bhuvur bhuvunta phooli daali, kiyoun jeevaan mur(u) maayay. Nanak Chayt(i) sehj(i) sookh(u) paavaiy, jo Hur(i), Vur ghur(i) dhun paayay.   5.   

Word Meaning:  Chayt = the first month (mid March – mid April).   Busunt = spring season.  Bhulaa = pleasant.   Bhuvur = bumblebees.    Soohaavrray = look beautiful.  Bun = forests.  Phoolay = bloom.   Munjh = in.  Baar(i) = wilderness; jungle.  Baahourray = come; meet again. Bireh(i) =  due to separation.   Birodh(i) =  opposition (attacks) by sensual pleasures.  Chheejaiy = weakens; suffers.   Unk(i) = in heart.  Suheejaiy = endure.  Bhuvunta = hovers over.   Daali = flowers and new leaves.  Mur(u) = death; dead towards worldly attractions.   5. 

MEANING:  The month of Chayt is very pleasant because it is springtime, and the whole vegetation blooms in the forest. The bumblebees flying around look very attractive. To see such beauty of nature, my mind yearns to see its Creator, and I wish that He would visit me in my heart. How can a man enjoy the comfort of Waheguru’s presence in his heart when his sensual pleasures are his enemies, which oppose the visit of Waheguru in his heart?  Such a devotee suffers a weakening of his bodily and mental strength due to his separation from Waheguru.  Like a bumblebee, which, attracted to the fresh flowers, hovers around them, enjoys their sap, and leaves, I am also attracted to the worldly pleasures.  How can I kill my mind from being attracted to worldly attractions so as to enjoy a high spiritual life, oh my mother? (Answer): The devotee, who recites Waheguru’s Naam with love and dedication in the month of  Chayt, and enshrines Waheguru’s Naam in her heart, enjoys blissfulness in the high spiritual state, oh Nanak.   5. 

 

ਵੈਸਾਖੁ ਭਲਾ ਸਾਖਾ ਵੇਸ ਕਰੇ॥ ਧਨ ਦੇਖੈ ਹਰਿ ਦੁਆਰਿ ਆਵਹੁ ਦਇਆ ਕਰੇ॥ ਘਰਿ ਆਉ ਪਿਆਰੇ ਦੁਤਰ ਤਾਰੇ ਤੁਧੁ ਬਿਨੁ ਅਢੁ ਨ ਮੋਲੋ॥ ਕੀਮਤਿ ਕਉਣੁ ਕਰੇ ਤੁਧੁ ਭਾਵਾ ਦੇਖਿ ਦਿਖਾਵੈ ਢੋਲੋ॥ ਦੂਰਿ ਨ ਜਾਨਾ ਅੰਤਰਿ ਮਾਨਾ ਹਰਿ ਕਾ ਮਹਲੁ ਪਛਾਨਾ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਵੈਸਾਖੀ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਭਾਵੈ ਸੁਰਤਿ ਸਬਦਿ ਮਨੁ ਮਾਨਾ॥6॥

Vaisaakh(u) bhulaa, shaakhaan vays kuray. Dhun daykhay Hur(i) Dooaar(i), aavhou duyya kuray. Ghur(i) aaou Piyaray, dootur taaray, Toodh bin(u) uddh(u) na molo. Keemut(i) kaun(u) kuray Toodh(u) bhaavaan, daykh(i) dikhaavaiy Dholo. Door(i) na jaana, untar(i) maana, Hur(i) ka Mehl(u) puchhaana. Nanak Vaisaakheen Prubh bhaavaiy, soort(i), Shabad(i) mun(u) maana.  6. 

Word Meaning:  Vaisaakh = the second month of Indian Calendar (Mid April – mid May).  Shaakhaan = vegetation.     Vays Kuray = wear different looks.  Hur(i) Dooaar(i) = Waheguru’s Door.   Dootur = the world sea, which is very difficult to swim across. Uddh(u) = half a cowrie shell.          Dholo = Dear (Waheguru).   Soort(i) = focus of mind.  6.

MEANING:  The month of Vaisakh(u) is also very pleasant as the vegetation wears many different attractive colours. But, despite the charming beauty of nature, a devotee does not find peace of mind when she is disconnected from Waheguru, and prays at His Door asking Him to visit her heart as she is not worth even half a cowrie-shell without Him; and it is only He who can ferry her across the difficult-to-swim world-sea.  Who will be able to tell my worth (I will become invaluable) if the Guru, who is connected with You all the time, can show me Your sight, and You come to like me? 

Then, I will believe that You reside in my heart, and are not away from me, and will know the place where You live (everywhere), oh Waheguru. In the pleasant month of Vaisakh, Waheguru likes the devotee who focuses her mind on Him with Guru’s Shabad, oh Nanak.6.   

 

ਮਾਹੁ ਜੇਠੁ ਭਲਾ ਪ੍ਰੀਤਮੁ ਕਿਉ ਬਿਸਰੈ॥ ਥਲ ਤਾਪਹਿ ਸਰ ਭਾਰ ਸਾਧਨ ਬਿਨਉ ਕਰੈ॥ ਧਨ ਬਿਨਉ ਕਰੇਦੀ ਗੁਣ ਸਾਰੇਦੀ ਗੁਣ ਸਾਰੀ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਭਾਵਾ॥ ਸਾਚੈ ਮਹਲਿ ਰਹੈ ਬੈਰਾਗੀ ਆਵਣ ਦੇਹਿ ਤਾ ਆਵਾ॥ ਨਿਮਾਣੀ ਨਿਤਾਣੀ ਹਰਿ ਬਿਨੁ ਕਿਉ ਪਾਵੈ ਸੁਖੁ ਮਹਲੀ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਜੇਠਿ ਜਾਣੈ ਤਿਸੁ ਜੈਸੀ ਕਰਮਿ ਮਿਲੈ ਗੁਣ ਗਹਲੀ॥7

Maah(u) Jaytth(u) bhulaa, Preetum(u) kiyoun bisraiy. Thul taapeh(i) sur bhaar, sadhun binou kuraiy. Dhun binou kuraydi, Goon saarayndi, Goon saareen Prubh bhaavaan. Saachaiy Mehl(i) ruhhaiy Baiyraagi, aavun dayh(i) ta aavaan. Nimaani, nitaani, Hur(i) bin(u) kiyoun paavaiy sookh(u( Mehleen. Nanak Jaytth(i) jaanaiy Tis(u) jaiysi, kerm(i) milaiy Goon gehli.   7.

Word Meaning:  Maah(u) = month. Jaytth(u) = the third month.  Bhulaa = pleasant. Taapeh(i) = heats up.  Sur = like. Bhaar = kiln; furnace.  Sadhun = the woman (devotee). Goon = Waheguru’s virtues. Saarayndi = remember; sing praise.  Baiyraagi = detached from the world (Waheguru).  Nimaani = without any pride; humble. Nitaani = powerless.  Goon Gehli = having acquired the noble qualities.   7.

MEANING:   Even the hot month of Jaytth(u) – the third month – in which the earth becomes hot like a furnace, becomes pleasant to the devotee who does not forget Waheguru.  Remembering Waheguru’s noble qualities, the devotee prays to Waheguru that He likes her when she sings His praise.  She knows that Waheguru, which is fully detached from worldly affairs, resides in the Palace where only high morality, purity, and justice prevail; and unless she has those qualities, Waheguru will not let her in.  But she cannot enjoy any comfort even in luxurious mansions without Waheguru, and without whom she feels to have no self-pride, and no energy. Only a devotee, who has noble qualities like Waheguru, is recognized in His Court, and it is with His benevolence that she can be connected with Him if she has acquired the noble qualities, oh Nanak. 7. 

 

ਆਸਾੜੁ ਭਲਾ ਸੂਰਜਿ ਗਗਨਿ ਤਪੈ॥ ਧਰਤੀ ਦੂਖ ਸਹੈ ਸੋਖੈ ਅਗਨਿ ਭਖੈ॥ ਅਗਨਿ ਰਸੁ ਸੋਖੈ ਮਰੀਐ ਧੋਖੈ ਭੀ ਸੋ ਕਿਰਤ ਨ ਹਾਰੇ॥ ਰਥੁ ਫਿਰੈ ਛਾਇਆ ਧਨ ਤਾਕੈ ਟੀਡੁ ਲਵੈ ਮੰਝਿ ਬਾਰੇ॥ ਅਵਗਣ ਬਾਧਿ ਚਲੀ ਦੁਖੁ ਆਗੈ ਸੁਖੁ ਤਿਸੁ ਸਾਚੁ ਸਮਾਲੇ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਜਿਸ ਨੋ ਇਹੁ ਮਨੁ ਦੀਆ ਮਰਣੁ ਜੀਵਣੁ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਨਾਲੇ॥ 8॥

Aasaarr(u) bhulaa, sooruj(i) guggun(i) tupaiy. Dherti dookh suhhaiy, sokhaiy ugun(i) bhukhaiy.  Ugun(i) rus(u) sokhaiy, muriyaiy dhokhaiy, bhee so kirt na haaray. Ruth(u) phiraiy, chhaaya dhun taakaiy, teed(u) luvvaiy munjh baaray. Uvgun bandh(i) chullie dookh(u) aagaiy, sookh(u) tis(u), Saach(u) smaalay. Nanak, Jis nu eh(u) mun(u) deeya, mern(u), jeevun(u) Prubh naalay.    8.

Word Meaning:  Aasaarr(u) =  Haarr(u) = the fourth month.  Guggu(i) = in the sky.  Dookh Suhhaiy = suffers in heat.  Sokahiy = absorbs.  Bhukhaiy = heats up.   Rus(u) = moisture.  Dhokhaiy = smoulders slowly.   Kirt = duty.    Ruth(u) Phiray = the sun goes higher in the sky (according to the Puranas, the sun rides a chariot which is pulled by a lame god – Arun).    Chhaaya = shade; protection.   Teed(u) = crickets.    Luvvaiy = make sounds.    Baaray = in the jungle.   Bundh(i) = package (accumulation) of ignoble qualities.  Smaalay = keeps in mind.   Mern(u) Jeevun = death and life.  8. 

MEANING:  In the month of Aasaarr(u), the sun heats up in the sky as a result of which the earth suffers badly as it absorbs the heat of the sun.  The sun's intense heat slowly absorbs moisture from the earth, which smoulders. Even though the earth suffers, it still performs its duty (as our mother) and protects us by providing the shade of trees and the cold water of rivers.   When the sun goes higher in the sky, the crickets make sounds in the forest to get relief from the heat, and the humans seek shade, but the devotees seek Waheguru’s shelter to protect them from the heat (fire) of worldly desires.  Those who keep Waheguru in mind feel blissful even in such a hot month, but trouble lies ahead for those who accumulate ignoble qualities.  Those who have surrendered their minds to Waheguru feel spiritual death when they are disconnected from Him, and thrive spiritually when they are connected with Him, oh Nanak.  8.

 

ਸਾਵਣਿ ਸਰਸ ਮਨਾ ਘਣ ਵਰਸਹਿ ਰੁਤਿ ਆਏ॥ ਮੈ ਮਨਿ ਤਨਿ ਸਹੁ ਭਾਵੈ ਪਿਰ ਪਰਦੇਸਿ ਸਿਧਾਏ॥ ਪਿਰੁ ਘਰਿ ਨਹੀ ਆਵੈ ਮਰੀਐ ਹਾਵੈ ਦਾਮਨਿ ਚਮਕਿ ਡਰਾਏ॥ ਸੇਜ ਇਕੇਲੀ ਖਰੀ ਦੁਹੇਲੀ ਮਰਣੁ ਭਇਆ ਦੁਖੁ ਮਾਏ॥ ਹਰਿ ਬਿਨੁ ਨੀਦ ਭੂਖ ਕਹੁ ਕੈਸੀ ਕਾਪੜੁ ਤਨਿ ਨ ਸੁਖਾਵਏ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਸਾ ਸੋਹਾਗਣਿ ਕੰਤੀ ਪਿਰ ਕੈ ਅੰਕਿ ਸਮਾਵਏ॥  9॥

MEANING:  After the very hot summer months of Jaytth(u) and Aasaarr(u), the rainy season sets in the month of Saavun, and every living being feels joyful.   However, the devotee who loves Waheguru by body and by mind does not feel blissful even in the beautiful month of Saavun when He is away from the devotee (she is not connected with Him).  Like a married woman whose husband is away, she does not enjoy the rainy season in his absence and sobs in misery; even beautiful lightning scares her. The devotee feels sad when the bed of her heart is empty. She feels miserable without Waheguru, and suffers as if she were dead, and shares her feelings of low spirit with her mother. She has sleepless nights and does not feel hungry.  Even the rich clothes offer her no comfort. But lucky is the devotee, who is loved by her Waheguru-Husband, and who rests comfortably in His Lap (has His Naam enshrined in her heart),  oh Nanak. 9.

 

ਭਾਦਉ ਭਰਮਿ ਭੁਲੀ ਭਰਿ ਜੋਬਨਿ ਪਛੁਤਾਣੀ॥ ਜਲ ਥਲ ਨੀਰਿ ਭਰੇ ਬਰਸ ਰੁਤੇ ਰੰਗੁ ਮਾਣੀ॥ ਬਰਸੈ ਨਿਸਿ ਕਾਲੀ ਕਿਉ ਸੁਖੁ ਬਾਲੀ ਦਾਦਰ ਮੋਰ ਲਵੰਤੇ॥ ਪ੍ਰਿਉ ਪ੍ਰਿਉ ਚਵੈ ਬਬੀਹਾ ਬੋਲੈ ਭੁਇਅੰਗਮ ਫਿਰਹਿ ਡਸੰਤੇ॥ ਮਛਰ ਡੰਗ ਸਾਇਰ ਭਰ ਸੁਭਰ ਬਿਨੁ ਹਰਿ ਕਿਉ ਸੁਖੁ ਪਾਈਐ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਪੂਛਿ ਚਲਉ ਗੁਰ ਅਪੁਨੇ ਜਹ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਤਹ ਹੀ ਜਾਈਐ॥ 10॥

MEANING:  In the prime of her youth, the woman (human) is misled by the illusions of Maya in the attractive month of Bhaadoun, when she enjoys the beauties of life in the rainy season, when the lakes and the fields are full of water; but she has to repent for the enjoyment of her sensual pleasures. On the other hand, the devotee of Waheguru does not feel comfort even in the lovely rainy season when it rains, and when the frogs and the peacocks make sounds, and the night is dark.  When she hears the rainbird chanting “priyo (dear), priyo”, and hears the sound of waves on the surface of the water of the lakes, which are full to the brim, she misses Waheguru, and feels the pain of separation from Him as if the snakes are biting, and the mosquitoes are stinging her. In other words, even though the environment is very colourful, the devotees do not enjoy it without Waheguru.  I will ask my Satguru for advice and to follow the path that leads to union with Waheguru, oh Nanak.   10.

MEANING:  With the arrival of the month of Assun(i), the hot season is over, and it is the beginning of the cool fall season. They feel disheartened with their weakened body.  They also repent that they did not serve Waheguru, and are running out of time to worship Him, so they pray to Waheguru to appear in their heart.  But when they see that the trees, and some perennial crops are still green, they pick up courage, and believe that as per the saying that the fruit which takes longer to ripe is sweet (or patience is virtue),  I will be patient, and pray to Waheguru to put me in the shelter of the Guru which, as the mediator,  will connect me with Him, oh Nanak.  11. 

 

ਕਤਕਿ ਕਿਰਤੁ ਪਇਆ ਜੋ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਪਾਇਆ॥ ਦੀਪਕੁ ਸਹਜਿ ਬਲੈ ਤਤਿ ਜਲਾਇਆ॥ ਦੀਪਕ ਰਸ ਤੇਲੋ ਧਨ ਪਿਰ ਮੇਲੋ ਧਨ ਓਮਾਹੈ ਸਰਸੀ॥ ਅਵਗਣ ਮਾਰੀ ਮਰੈ ਨ ਸੀਝੈ ਗੁਣਿ ਮਾਰੀ ਤਾ ਮਰਸੀ॥ ਨਾਮੁ ਭਗਤਿ ਦੇ ਨਿਜ ਘਰਿ ਬੈਠੇ ਅਜਹੁ ਤਿਨਾੜੀ ਆਸਾ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਮਿਲਹੁ ਕਪਟ ਦਰ ਖੋਲਹੁ ਏਕ ਘੜੀ ਖਟੁ ਮਾਸਾ॥12॥

MEANING:  Based on the results of his previous deeds, one performs the kind of deeds for which he is predestined by Waheguru. (Placing them on big leaves, the Hindu women float lighted earthen lamps in flowing waters as a ritual, but it is of no spiritual significance.) But the devotees, who make a connection with Waheguru, light up the lamp of the Divine Knowledge in their peaceful mind (above the effects of Maya).  The blissfulness of the union with Waheguru in the mind of the zestful devotee serves as the oil in the lamp.  Because of her ignoble qualities, the spiritually dead woman does not succeed (to achieve union with Waheguru) in this life, but the devotee, who acquires noble qualities, symbolically dies to worldly desires (kills them).  Those whom Waheguru gives His Naam and His worship, stay in their own mind (their mind does not rush for worldly attractions), and always long for Waheguru’s sight.  They pray to Him that He appear in their minds soon and open the strongly resistant door of their minds (to see Him with the eyes of their minds), as they already feel that even a small moment feels like a period of six months in His separation, oh, Nanak.   12.

Soon(i) = listening to.  Bunkay = handsome.  Sur(i) = in (holy) pools.   Gung = the River Ganga.  Bayni = Tribayni = the three Rivers – Ganga, Yamuna, and Sarasvati.  Sungum =  the merging.  Saat Smoond = the seven seas of the world.   Poonn = noble deeds.  Jaata = recognized.  Muhaa Rus(u) = the great bliss.  Utth-Sutth(i) = 68 holy places. 15.

MEANING:  (The Hindus believe the first day of Maagh to be holy, and consider it to be a deed of great spiritual worth to take dips at holy places to purify their mind); but the whole month of Maagh becomes holy for the devotees who recognize (make connection with) Waheguru in their mind, which itself becomes a holy place.  When a devotee acquires noble qualities of Waheguru in his/her heart, the Waheguru-Friend comes to reside in their heart in their peaceful mind.  By listening to the virtues of the handsome and the dear Lord, the devotee acquires those virtues in her heart, and prays before Him that with His blessings, her heart could become the real holy place with His presence in there, where not only the three Rivers – Ganga, Yamuna, and Sarasvati – merge into one another, but also all the seven sees of the world will seem to be present (or the five senses, the mind and the wisdom in the body become pure and a holy place) and she could take a dip in her own heart rather than going to any supposedly holy place. For the devotee, who recognizes (makes connection with) Waheguru, which is the same in each eon, all the noble deeds, donations to Brahmans, and the ritualistic worship, are subsumed in His worship (the devotees do not need them).   The whole month becomes a great bliss for the devotees, who recite Waheguru’s Naam, which is like taking dips at all the 68 holy places, oh Nanak.  15.

 

ਫਲ਼ਗੁਨਿ ਮਨਿ ਰਹਸੀ ਪ੍ਰੇਮੁ ਸੁਭਾਇਆ॥ ਅਨਦਿਨੁ ਰਹਸੁ ਭਇਆ ਆਪੁ ਗਵਾਇਆ॥ ਮਨ ਮੋਹੁ ਚੁਕਾਇਆ ਜਾ ਤਿਸੁ ਭਾਇਆ ਕਰਿ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਘਰਿ ਆਓ॥ ਬਹੁਤੇ ਵੇਸ ਕਰੀ ਪਿਰ ਬਾਝਹੁ ਮਹਲੀ ਲਹਾ ਨ ਥਾਓ॥ ਹਾਰ ਡੋਰ ਰਸ ਪਾਟ ਪਟੰਬਰ ਪਿਰਿ ਲੋੜੀ ਸੀਗਾਰੀ॥ ਨਾਨਕ ਮੇਲਿ ਲਈ ਗੁਰਿ ਅਪਣੈ ਘਰਿ ਵਰੁ ਪਾਇਆ ਨਾਰੀ॥ 16॥

Phulgoon(i) mun(i) rehsi, praym(u) soobhaaya. Undin(u) rehus(u) bhuyya, aap(u) guvaaya. Word Meaning:  Phulgoon(i) = (in the) twelfth month. Rehsi = blooming of heart. Soobhaaya = with noble intentions.    Aap(u) = selfishness and ego.  Guvaaya = gotten rid of.             Ghur(i) = in heart.  Aaou = come.   Vays = religious show offs.  Mehleen = in His Palace.   Luhaan = acquire.  Haar Door = garland of flowers and pearls, etc.  Paat Putumbur = silk clothes.  Lorri = liked.   Vur(u) = husband (Waheguru).   16.

MEANING: The devotee, who loves Waheguru with noble intentions, enjoys bliss. It becomes a blissful day and night for the devotee who kills her selfishness and ego (haumein).  With Waheguru’s blessing, she gets rid of (overcomes) her worldly attachments, and Waheguru appears in her heart with His benevolence.  An individual who adorns herself with many religious symbols without real love for Him gets no room in Waheguru’s Palace.  But the devotee who is liked by Waheguru feels as if she has been adorned with a necklace of pearls, a garland of flowers, and with silk clothes. Her Guru connects her with Waheguru-Husband in her own heart, oh Nanak.   16. 

 

ਬੇ ਦਸ ਮਾਹ ਰੁਤੀ ਥਿਤੀ ਵਾਰ ਭਲੇ॥ ਘੜੀ ਮੂਰਤ ਪਲ ਸਾਚੇ ਆਏ ਸਹਜਿ ਮਿਲੇ॥ ਪ੍ਰਭ ਮਿਲੇ ਪਿਆਰੇ ਕਾਰਜ ਸਾਰੇ ਕਰਤਾ ਸਭ ਬਿਧਿ ਜਾਣੈ॥ ਜਿਨਿ ਸੀਗਾਰੀ ਤਿਸਹਿ ਪਿਆਰੀ ਮੇਲੁ ਭਇਆ ਰੰਗੁ ਮਾਣੈ॥ ਘਰਿ ਸੇਜ ਸੁਹਾਵੀ ਜਾ ਪਿਰਿ ਰਾਵੀ ਗੁਰਮੁਖਿ ਮਸਤਕਿ ਭਾਗੋ॥

Bay-dus, maah, rootie, thitee, vaar bhulay.  Ghurri, moorut, pul, saachay, aayay sehj(i) milay. Prubh milay Piyaray, kaaruj saaray, kerta subh bidh(i) jaanaiy.  Jinh(i) sheegaari, Tiseh(i) piyari, mayl(u) bhuyya rung(u) maanaiy.  Ghur(i) sayj soohaavi, ja Pir(i) raavi, Gurmukh(i) bhaago.

Word Meaning:  Bay-Dus = two and ten = 12.  Maah = months.   Rootie = seasons.  Thitee = phases of the moon.    Vaar = days of the week.       Bhulay = good; pleasant.  Ghurri, Moorut, Pul = small time periods.  Saachay = (become) eternal.  Kaaruj = tasks.  Saaray = completed successfully.  Bidh(i) = means.  Sheegaari = adorned.  Rung(u) Maanay = enjoy the love of union.  Sayj = the bed (of heart).  Soohaavi = comforting. Bhaago = good fortune.  Ehi = day.  Nis(i) = night.  Raavaiy = enjoys (union).   Vur(u) = Husband.  Thir(u) = permanent.  Soohaago = married life.   17. 

MEANING:  For the devotee, who is connected with Waheguru in her peaceful state of mind, all the twelve months, all seasons, all phases of the moon, all days of the week, and all the short periods of time, become eternally very pleasant. Waheguru knows all the ways to connect a devotee with Him, and the tasks of the devotee get completed successfully. Waheguru, who adorns His devotee, loves her, and she enjoys the love of her union with Him.  With her good fortune and with the grace of the Guru, the heart-bed of the devotee becomes very comforting for her when Waheguru enjoys her union.  Waheguru enjoys her union (stays connected with her) day and night, and she acquires the eternal Husband, oh Nanak.   17.1.
